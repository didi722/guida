The most Venetian way of eating is also the simplest: **step into a bacaro, order a few cicchetti and an ombra, and eat standing at the counter**.

## The minimum vocabulary
- **Bacaro**: the Venetian tavern, often tiny, with a counter full of small bites.
- **Cicchetti**: small snacks, often on a slice of bread or in a paper cone. Creamed salt cod, sweet-and-sour sardines, meatballs, crostini, vegetables: you choose by looking at the counter.
- **Ombra**: a small glass of wine. Tradition says the name comes from the shadow of St Mark's bell tower, which followed the wine sellers during the day: legend or history, the ritual remains.
- **Giro di ombre**: hopping from one bacaro to the next, tasting a little in each.

## A few practical rules
1. **Step away from the main routes.** Two or three bridges are enough: prices fall and quality rises. Cannaregio, Castello and Dorsoduro are great starting points.
2. **Go at the right times.** Counters are fullest in the late morning and early afternoon, then again at aperitivo time.
3. **Be wary of menus with photographs** and of anyone calling you in from the street.
4. **Ask.** If you do not know a cicchetto, ask what it is: nine times out of ten the owner will be glad to recommend something.
5. **Take your time**, but do not expect table service: here you order at the counter.
